RESEARCH DATA POLICY

1. Every text record must identify its source, edition or witness, language, licence, and transcription type.
2. An edited text must never be labelled as a direct manuscript transcription.
3. A manuscript must not be described as containing a passage unless that witness actually preserves the passage.
4. Dead Sea Scrolls are Hebrew/Aramaic witnesses. They are not witnesses to the Apostolic Writings.
5. Reconstructed, damaged, supplied, or uncertain readings must be marked explicitly.
6. Lost works may be catalogued from surviving references, but their missing contents must never be fabricated.
7. Translation output must identify its source or engine, date, and human-review status.
8. Public-domain or compatible licensed data only. Preserve attribution and licence notices in the app.
9. The present bundled Hebrew and Greek texts are research starting editions, not a complete manuscript archive or critical apparatus.

10. TEXT-FIRST DEVELOPMENT PRIORITY
    Complete the searchable text corpus before undertaking manuscript-photo or facsimile integration.
    Prioritize original-language transcriptions, manuscript identifiers, passage coverage, fragment/column/line coordinates, editorial marks, and source/licence metadata.
    Build exact-word and exact-phrase search over the integrated text, with witness filters and clear separation of manuscript text, editorial reconstruction, and comparison witnesses.
    Photographs and facsimiles are deferred until a later app update after the text archive is substantially complete. Their absence must not block text ingestion, verification, indexing, or search.
    Do not describe the text archive as complete until coverage has been inventoried and gaps, unavailable texts, and unverified readings are explicitly recorded.

11. COMPLETE APOCRYPHA BOOK TEXTS
    Import complete available Greek editions of the Apocrypha and Deuterocanonical writings as whole readable books before expanding individual fragment concordances.
    The current Greek collection is Henry Barclay Swete's Septuagint as digitally transcribed by Open Greek and Latin First1KGreek and distributed through nathans/lxx-swete under CC BY-SA 4.0. Preserve attribution and share-alike terms.
    Label these records as edited Greek edition texts, not diplomatic transcriptions of one ancient manuscript. Keep Old Greek and Theodotionic forms of Daniel, Susanna, and Bel and the Dragon separate.
    Do not substitute Greek editions for surviving Hebrew or Aramaic witnesses. Add DSS and other ancient-language witnesses as distinct records and compare them only after passage-level verification.
    The Swete Greek source does not contain 2 Esdras / 4 Ezra; the archive imports a separate Latin transmission text under section 12. Do not imply that a Greek original is extant or imported. Do not fabricate missing portions or imply the current imported collection exhausts every canon or manuscript tradition.
    Retain collection boundaries and original numbering for Odes and Psalm 151. The Prayer of Manasseh (Odes 8) may be separately indexed for navigation, but its original Odes references must remain attached. The Greek additions to Daniel remain represented within the separate Daniel textual forms.
12. LATIN TRANSMISSION TEXTS
    The archive includes 2 Esdras / 4 Ezra and the Prayer of Manasseh in Latin forms from the Latin Library digital collection, collected by CLTK and identified as public domain by the source.
    The 16-chapter 2 Esdras text is composite: 5 Ezra (chapters 1–2), 4 Ezra proper (chapters 3–14), and 6 Ezra (chapters 15–16). Keep these literary divisions visible in research notes.
    The Latin text is a transmitted version, not the lost Greek original or a direct manuscript transcription. Preserve source wording and numbering, and compare against critical editions and manuscript witnesses before making textual claims.
    The Prayer of Manasseh is represented in both the Greek form separately indexed from Odes 8 and a Latin transmitted form. Keep these as distinct textual forms; do not assume either is the original language or conflate them.
